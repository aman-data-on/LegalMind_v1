# Content-first contract tests — five synthetic agreements

**Status: 📁 ANALYSIS — a test record, not a specification.** It decides nothing and
amends no locked decision. First run **15 September 2026**.

`AM-51` (2026-09-09) and `AM-60` (2026-09-13) made applicability a question about what a
document **contains**, not what its label says. Nothing exercised that on a realistic
agreement: the `STRUCTURAL` fixture is one short synthetic clause set, and the real
counterparty paper is MSA/TOS/NDA-shaped and gitignored (54.6).

Five synthetic agreements of four to five pages now do, run through the **live API** —
login, upload, review, analyze, findings — so what is measured is the shipped path.

---

## What these documents are, and are not

They are **test inputs**. Every party in them is invented; none is a real company. They
state no organisational legal position, and nothing in them is a Company Standard, a
Legal Rule or a threshold (rule 21).

Every figure is chosen to land on a **known side of a ratified standard**, so the expected
outcome is *derived from the Constitution's own number* rather than asserted. The
generator records the expectation beside the clause that produces it.

Generated by [`backend/tools/generate_test_contracts.py`](../../backend/tools/generate_test_contracts.py)
into `legal-docs/` — **gitignored**, exactly as `e2e_bootstrap.py` does for its
`STRUCTURAL` fixture. CI job 8 rejects a contract file entering the repository; only the
generator is committed. Run by
[`backend/tools/run_contract_tests.py`](../../backend/tools/run_contract_tests.py).

## The five cases

| | Document | Declared type | Designed to produce |
|---|---|---|---|
| **C1** | Managed Services Agreement | `MSA` | every applicable clause **Acceptable** |
| **C2** | Strategic Partner Agreement | `OTHER` | **the same clauses, the same answers** |
| **C3** | Technology Services Agreement | `MSA` | Acceptable **and** Requires modification |
| **C4** | Vendor Services and Confidentiality Agreement | `OTHER` | MSA **and** NDA clauses in one document, mixed outcomes |
| **C5** | Distribution and Reseller Agreement | `OTHER` | an **uncapped liability** term → Needs a decision |

**C2 is the point of the exercise.** It carries byte-identical clauses to C1 under a
different label. If the two score differently, the document type is still deciding
something it is no longer allowed to decide.

C3 moves three figures off the Constitution's number — payment 45 days against 21, cure
15 days against 30, confidentiality survival 2 years against 3 — and leaves the rest on
it. C4 mixes families deliberately: an `OTHER`-labelled document carrying both MSA
payment/termination clauses and an NDA non-solicitation term at 1 year against the
ratified 2.

C5 carries a clause accepting **unlimited liability**, which Constitution §9 names an
Unacceptable Position. `constitution_boundaries.py` fires only on
`cap_status == "UNLIMITED"`, and `AM-63` routes a prohibited term to **Needs a decision**
rather than Requires modification.

## Measured results

Numbers below are stated with the run that produced them, because the contract set changed
once mid-loop (the billing-dispute clause was removed — see gap 3) and the two vocabulary
fixes are **not yet published to production**, so the live snapshot still pins the old
terminology.

| Run | Contract set | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | with dispute clause | C1 **25 findings, 17/19**; C2 **25 findings, 17/19 — identical to C1**; C3 25/17-of-19; C4 22 findings, 8/10; C5 21 findings, 4/7 |
| **B** | dispute clause removed | C1 **25 findings, 17/18** — `PAYMENT-PERIOD-MSA-001` now correct, confirming gap 3 was the cause |

Run B stopped after C1: the account's analysis budget (`ANALYSIS = 30 requests/hour/user`,
`api/ratelimit.py`) was exhausted by the loop's repeated runs. That limit is a real control
and the harness backs off against it rather than circumventing it; a clean full-set run
needs a fresh hour.

**Still failing against production, expected:** `CONF-SURVIVAL-MSA-001` and the C5
`LIABILITY-MSA-001` case. Both are the two vocabulary gaps fixed in the standard files but
not yet imported and published — the fixes are proven at extraction level (below) and by
`tests/test_contract_recognition_gaps.py`, not yet end to end through the live API.

## What the applicability engine got right

**Content-first works.** C1 (`MSA`) and C2 (`OTHER`) produced **25 findings each and
identical statuses on every requirement**. The label changed nothing — which is precisely
what `AM-51`/`AM-60` require and what had never been demonstrated on a realistic document.

Applicability also reached across families on content, as designed: an `OTHER`-labelled
agreement containing a confidentiality survival term was measured against the NDA
standard, and one containing a data-retrieval window against the TOS standard. Those are
not spurious findings — the clauses are genuinely present.

## Three recognition gaps this surfaced

All three are **vocabulary, not logic**. The comparison, classification and routing behaved
correctly throughout; what failed was the engine's ability to recognise ordinary contract
English. Each is pinned in
[`backend/tests/test_contract_recognition_gaps.py`](../../backend/tests/test_contract_recognition_gaps.py).

### 1 · An uncapped liability term was not recognised — FIXED

**Severity: this is the one that mattered.** `LIABILITY-MSA-001`/`-TOS-001` held
`unlimited_phrases` of only `"shall be unlimited"` and `"liability shall not be limited"`.
C5's clause — *"Each party accepts unlimited liability…"*, *"is not capped and shall not be
subject to any financial limit"* — matched neither, so extraction returned
`cap_status: ABSENT`.

The consequence was not cosmetic. §9's boundary fires only on `UNLIMITED`, so an agreement
accepting unlimited liability was classified **MISSING** and surfaced as *"Requires
modification"* — a **prohibited term shown as a drafting nit** instead of *"Needs a
decision"*.

Measured before and after, on the shipped extractor:

```
before:  [('ABSENT',    None, None, None)]
after :  [('UNLIMITED', None, None, None)]
```

**Fix, in two parts — and the second was only found by the full-set run.**

Seven phrasings were added to `extraction.unlimited_phrases`, and the extraction unit test
passed. It passed because it handed the clause straight to `extract_liability_facts`. The
live pipeline **maps first**, and there the uncapped clause scored **2 against a confirm
threshold of 5** — the only thing it matched was the section heading. So the clause was never
recognised as a liability clause, extraction never ran on it, and the Finding came back
MISSING.

An uncapped liability term was therefore **invisible, not merely misread**. The same phrasings
were added to `mapping_rules.exact_phrases`, and precision was checked in both directions: a
normally capped clause still confirms, an unrelated clause still does not.

This is the clearest argument in this record for running the whole pipeline rather than the
unit under test: a green unit test sat on top of a defect that made a Not-Negotiable term
disappear.

### 2 · "following the termination" left the basis unattributed — FIXED

`CONF-SURVIVAL-MSA-001`/`-NDA-001` held basis terms `"after termination"`,
`"post-termination"`, `"from the date of termination"` — but not
**`"following the termination"`**, which is at least as common in drafting.

The **value** extracted correctly (3 years); only the basis came back `null`, which is
`UNABLE_TO_EVALUATE` and fails closed to *"Needs a decision"*. A clause sitting exactly on
the Constitution's number was sent to a human as unreadable.

**Fix:** six phrasings added to the basis vocabulary. No position changed.

### 3 · A billing-dispute window collides with the payment period — NOT FIXED, recorded

`PAYMENT-PERIOD-MSA-001` claims `cap_phrases` of `"days of the invoice date"`,
`"days of invoice"` **and** `"days of receipt of the invoice"`, with a single basis term:
`"invoice"`. It therefore claims essentially **any** *"within N days … invoice"*
construction.

A billing-dispute window is ordinary drafting and is indistinguishable to it. With both
clauses present the evaluator holds two values for one basis — 21 and 15 — and classifies
`CONFLICT`, surfacing as *"Needs a decision"* on a payment term that is exactly on the
Constitution's number.

**This is fail-closed and therefore safe** (rule 15): nothing is asserted acceptable, the
evidence is kept, and a person decides. It is **recorded rather than fixed**, because
narrowing the vocabulary trades a false alarm for *missed payment clauses* — a recall loss
in the unsafe direction. Choosing that trade is an owner decision about ratified
configuration, not a session's.

The dispute clause is therefore left out of the five contracts, and the collision is
reproduced on its own in the test file. `DISPUTE-WINDOW-MSA-001` is consequently **not
exercised** by this suite.

## A fourth finding, outside the engine

**A session can be briefly unusable immediately after login.** A `GET` issued in the same
second as a successful `POST /auth/login` returned `401` while the session row existed,
and succeeded on the next attempt. Reproduced across several runs; the harness now polls
`/auth/session` and reports which attempt succeeded rather than hiding it.

Not diagnosed further here — it is an authentication-path question, not an analysis one,
and it is recorded so the next person meets a note instead of a mystery.

Two harness lessons worth keeping: `analyze` returns `mode="queued"` in production and the
work goes to the Celery worker, so reading findings immediately reports **"0 findings" for
a review the engine has not looked at yet** — indistinguishable from the engine finding
nothing, and exactly how the first run lied. And nginx rate-limits the `legalmind_auth`
zone, so a harness that logs in per request earns `503`s: log in once.

## Applying the two fixes

The corrected vocabulary is in the standard files. **Production is unaffected until the
standards are re-imported and a snapshot is published** — the live snapshot
`5c85b87c` pins the old terminology, and existing reviews keep it regardless (rule 16).

Positions were verified unchanged by assertion as the files were edited, and
`verify_terminology` reports **34 PASS · 6 FAIL**, identical to `main`'s baseline — the six
being the pre-existing AB-14 reconciliation failures, unrelated to this work.

## Reproducing

```bash
cd backend
python3 -m tools.generate_test_contracts                     # writes to legal-docs/
LEGALMIND_TEST_PASSWORD=… python3 -m tools.run_contract_tests --json-out /tmp/run.json
python3 -m pytest tests/test_contract_recognition_gaps.py    # the three gaps
```
